Detective novel · Chapter One

Blackout on Line 9

Genre: modern urban mysteryLength: ~900 words (excerpt)POV: close third, Detective Inés Varga

The city went dark at 11:52 p.m., all of it at once, like someone had leaned on the wrong switch.

Inés Varga was on the forty-first floor of police headquarters when it happened, halfway through a vending-machine coffee she already regretted. The skyline outside her window didn't fade; it vanished. Eleven million people, and for a second she could hear every one of them stop talking.

Then the sirens started, and her phone lit up with a name she didn't want to see at midnight.

"Varga." Commissioner Okafor never wasted words. "Line 9 depot. Bring your shoes that don't slip."

"Line 9 isn't even open yet."

"It is now," he said. "For one passenger."

— — —

The Line 9 depot sat under the old textile district, a cathedral of white tile and brand-new rails that still smelled of paint. The trains were driverless, the platforms glass-walled, the whole system run from a control room the brochures called the brain.

The brain was locked.

"Three keycards," said the night supervisor, a young man named Tomasz whose hands would not stop rubbing each other. "Mine, the chief engineer's and the security office. All three, at the same time. That's the rule. Nobody goes in alone."

"And yet," Inés said, looking through the reinforced window, "somebody did."

Daniel Reyes, chief engineer of the most expensive public project in the city's history, lay on his back between two rows of monitors. Every screen was black. His reading glasses were folded neatly on the desk, the way a man folds them when he means to come back for them.

"Cameras?" she asked.

"Down with the power. Forty minutes." Tomasz swallowed. "The backup generator should have kicked in after ninety seconds. It didn't."

"Should have," Inés repeated. In her experience, should have was where most murders lived.

— — —

The lights came back at 12:32. The room hummed awake around them, fans spinning up, monitors blinking through their start-up logos as if nothing had happened. On the main screen a map of Line 9 glowed green from end to end. Every train in its place. Every door closed. Every sensor reporting normal.

Inés didn't like rooms that insisted everything was normal.

The medical examiner, Dr. Halloran, knelt by the body and spoke without looking up. "No obvious wound. Lips slightly blue. I'll know more after the lab, but I'd say he stopped breathing sometime around midnight."

"During the blackout."

"During the blackout," Halloran agreed. "Convenient for somebody."

On the desk, beside the glasses, sat a thin printed report with a red stamp across the cover: SAFETY CERTIFICATION — APPROVED. Reyes's signature was at the bottom. The ink had smeared, as if it had been signed in a hurry, or by a hand that wasn't steady.

Inés photographed it, then photographed the keycard reader by the door. The log on its little screen showed three entries at 11:49 p.m., three minutes before the city went dark.

Tomasz's card. The security office card. And Daniel Reyes's own.

"You opened this door at eleven forty-nine?" she asked.

Tomasz went pale. "No. I was upstairs. I swear. My card was in my locker."

"And security?"

"There's nobody in security at night anymore," he said. "The system does it. That's the whole point of Line 9. The system does everything."

— — —

Outside, the city was loud again: horns, helicopters, a thousand phones filming a blackout that was already over. Inés stood on the empty platform and watched a driverless train glide in, stop exactly on its mark, open its doors to no one and close them again.

Somewhere in this city, someone had turned off the lights to commit a murder. Or someone had committed a murder and let the city take the blame.

Her phone buzzed. A message from an unknown number, sent at 11:51 p.m., delayed by the outage and only arriving now:

Ask him about the sensor on curve 14.

Inés looked down the tunnel, where the rails bent into the dark.

"Tomasz," she called back toward the control room. "How many sensors are there on curve fourteen?"

A pause. Then, uncertain: "One. Why?"

"Because," Inés said, "I think it's the only witness we've got."

Series outline (short)

Act I: the locked room and the impossible keycard log. Act II: a fired hacker is framed; Reyes is revealed to have hidden a crash risk on curve 14. Act III: the killer used smart-traffic data as an alibi, but the single curve-14 sensor recorded a train moving during the blackout.
